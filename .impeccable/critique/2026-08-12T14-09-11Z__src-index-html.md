## Design Specificity Verdict

The dark navy, glass-like treatment is coherent and calm, but still reads as a broadly interchangeable developer dashboard. Control Panel's distinctive value—safe, quick local-service operation—does not yet dominate the composition. The deterministic scan found 0 findings in `src/index.html`; this is a clean mechanical baseline, not proof of strong interaction hierarchy.

## Overall Impression

The surface is compact, macOS-appropriate, and already useful for routine starts. Its largest opportunity is to turn every project card from a small dashboard tile into an operationally legible control surface: state first, one obvious next action, evidence immediately available when something fails.

## What's Working

- Routine actions live alongside their project, which supports high-frequency local work.
- The scan-root workflow clearly states its discovery scope and preserves project ownership of lifecycle scripts.
- Modal behavior is sound: explicit close paths, Escape and backdrop dismissal, focus placement, and inline form errors.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Operational state is too quiet

**Why it matters:** A color-only dot forces recall and is inaccessible when the user needs to decide whether to start, stop, or recover a service.

**Fix:** Replace it with a textual badge such as “运行中 / 已停止 / 启动中 / 错误”; show a one-line failure or transition summary directly beneath the name when relevant.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P1] Every card presents too many equal-weight actions

**Why it matters:** Seven peer buttons make lifecycle actions compete with safe navigation and configuration. The dense action strip becomes hard to scan and horizontally constrained at normal desktop widths.

**Fix:** Make one context-sensitive lifecycle action primary; group restart and secondary operations under a labelled overflow or separate utility group.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] Project identity, purpose, and diagnostic evidence compete

**Why it matters:** The combined/truncated headline and hidden output obscure the information needed to recognize a project and understand failures quickly.

**Fix:** Give the project name its own headline, keep notes secondary, and render a labelled output/error snippet only for state changes or errors.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P2] The list will not scale gracefully to 10–30 projects

**Why it matters:** Three narrow columns plus horizontal action strips require card-by-card scanning; there is no visible search, sorting, or efficient keyboard path.

**Fix:** Introduce search and a state/sort control; use a two-column intermediate layout and consider compact operational rows for dense views.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P2] Labels blur global and project scopes

**Why it matters:** “目录” means scan roots in the top bar but Finder at card level; “配置” is likewise global or project-specific. This creates avoidable hesitation.

**Fix:** Rename global controls to “扫描目录” and “全局配置”; use “在 Finder 中打开” and “编辑项目展示信息” for project utilities, including menu labels/tooltips.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Daily local developer with 15+ services:** Without search, sorting, shortcuts, or readable state labels, the product becomes a card-scanning task rather than a glanceable control center.

**On-call maintainer / debugging user:** A red dot without direct failure output, timestamp, or recovery path leaves the most stressful moment least informative.

**New project owner:** Scope ambiguity in “目录 / 配置” and lifecycle guidance confined to settings make it unclear whether an action affects the panel, a scan root, or the project manifest.

## Minor Observations

- The status pill is `aria-hidden`, so its label is unavailable to assistive technology.
- Buttons lack visible keyboard focus styles.
- 10–12px text and low-contrast ghost actions are near the lower limit for fast scanning.
- The responsive grid jumps from three columns directly to one; a two-column middle breakpoint is missing.

## Questions to Consider

- Can a user diagnose and recover a failed service without opening details?
- Should a control panel behave more like compact operational rows with progressive disclosure than dashboard tiles?
- Which actions truly need to be visible on every project at all times?
